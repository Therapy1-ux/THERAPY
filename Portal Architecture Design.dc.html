Client Portal architecture — one portal, entitlement-unlocked.

Nothing implemented. Audit of what exists, the entitlement model, data governance boundaries, Momence source-of-truth map, phased plan, and challenges to the brief. Companion to the Comms Layer Design — they share one backend.

1 · Audit — what the portal is today

Member Portal v3 (this project) is a high-fidelity front-end prototype: dashboard, sessions, My Plan (cross-referenced strategies, at-home techniques), Devices & Records (wearable connect cards, bloodwork/scan upload zones). There is no authentication system to map — none exists. No backend, no database, no client login on yourtherapy.au beyond Momence's own hosted member login.

What this means for the brief: "evolve the existing portal" = keep the v3 screens, UX language and information design (they already answer the four first-screen questions: upcoming, progress, next step, membership — see §7). What gets built new is everything beneath: identity, entitlements, data stores. That's not a rebuild — the prototype was always the specification for this.

Retain: all v3 screens (re-skinned by entitlement, not forked), the bronze design language, Momence as commerce spine, the Integration Checklist's backend stack (same Postgres/auth/audit foundation serves portal + comms layer — build once). Refactor: v3's implicit "member-only" framing becomes the entitlement-gated view of the one portal.

2 · The entitlement model (core of the design)

One account (created at first visit, keyed to Momence customer ID). One entitlements table: account_id → entitlement key → source → granted_at → expires_at. Features check entitlement keys — never "is this person a member". Membership tiers, corporate deals, Seasons access, comp upgrades, trials: all just rows. Momence membership webhooks grant/revoke member entitlements automatically; staff can grant manually (audited).

portal.base — every client
Profile · book / upcoming / history · forms & consents · documents · comms preferences · clinician-shared notes & follow-ups · programs · notifications · membership info + join path
member.core — any tier
Tier + inclusions · credits used/remaining · member booking · benefits & events · progress dashboard · goals & check-ins · member content/offers

Seasons is another entitlement namespace on the same identity — a different interface, not a different account

3 · Longitudinal data & the internal/client boundary

History starts at first consented interaction, not at membership purchase. The record spine: account → records (typed: appointment, form, consent, document, metric, note, assessment) → each record carries provenance (source, author, date) and a visibility flag. Membership never creates data — it unlocks richer views over data that was accumulating all along.

Two views, one store. Internal clinical view (notes, observations, assessments, risks, clinician tasks) is visible only to authorised clinical roles. Client-facing view shows only records whose type is client-safe by governance rule AND, for anything clinical, has been explicitly clinician-approved for sharing — publication is an act, not a default. Nothing becomes client-visible because it exists.

Wearables write normalised metrics into this same store (per the Integration Checklist schema) — never a side silo. Recommended first integration: Oura via aggregator — best data quality for THERAPY's nervous-system positioning (HRV, readiness, temperature), server-side sync (unlike Apple Health), fastest approval (unlike Garmin), and the likeliest device among your early members. Apple Health second once the iOS-shell question is decided.

4 · Source-of-truth map (Momence's role)

Clients / identityMomence (customer record) — portal account references it, never duplicates name/contact editing
Appointments & bookingsMomence — portal reads via API/webhooks; booking actions go to Momence flows
Memberships & paymentsMomence — tier + status webhooks drive entitlement grants; money never touches our stack
Membership entitlementsOurs — Momence knows the product purchased; only our entitlement table knows what it unlocks
Forms & consentsOurs — health consent needs versioning, audit and granularity Momence isn't built for (intake forms may stay in Momence short-term)
Clinical / session notesOurs (governed health store) — never in Momence, never in the comms store
CommunicationsOurs — the comms layer's conversation store (per companion design)
Longitudinal health dataOurs — the governed record spine in §3; Momence data flows in as read-only provenance-tagged records

Rule: each fact lives in exactly one system; everything else holds references. Momence API capability (OAuth2, members/appointments endpoints, webhooks via Zapier or native) was verified in the comms design — the same sandbox test covers both projects.

5 · Seasons boundary

Shared: identity (one login), wearable connections (one OAuth per device, consumed by both), and consented summary-level history where the client explicitly opts in.

Separated — deliberately: THERAPY clinical records stay under the clinic entity (AHPRA/clinical governance); Seasons should hold its own product data under its own privacy policy. If Seasons is (or becomes) a separate legal entity, cross-entity data flow needs its own consent event and a data-sharing agreement — design the API boundary now, share nothing by default. Do not pre-merge databases in anticipation.

6 · Security, governance & items needing specialist confirmation

Same stack as the comms design (RBAC, least privilege, encryption, AU residency, immutable audit + access logging, consent ledger, retention controls) — one implementation serves both. Staff portal is a different permission set on the same infrastructure; clinical roles scoped to assigned clients; every sensitive read logged.

Needs legal/specialist confirmation before build: whether the record store constitutes a "health record" under Victorian Health Records Act (almost certainly yes → HPP obligations); APP 11 security requirements for health information; retention minimums for clinical notes (7 years+ in VIC, longer for minors); whether AI-derived insights are themselves health records; My Health Record interaction (likely none, confirm); Seasons entity structure.

8 · Where I challenge the brief

The base-portal feature list is too big for "base". Seventeen features for every casual client is practice-management-software energy — the opposite of §10's calm brief. Phase-1 base portal should be five things done beautifully: upcoming, book, history, profile, membership path. Forms/documents/programs arrive in Phase 2 when the sharing workflow exists.

"Basic progress information" for non-members is a trap. Progress requires interpreted data, which requires the clinician-approval workflow. Shipping a thin version early creates clinical-governance exposure for zero retention value. Give non-members follow-up recommendations (text a clinician wrote) — call nothing "progress" until the governed pipeline exists.

Don't build forms twice. If Momence intake forms are adequate today, keep them there for Phase 1 and migrate when the consent ledger ships — building our forms engine in parallel with Momence's is exactly the double-handling the brief warns against.

Entitlement flexibility has a cost. A fully generic entitlement engine invites configuration sprawl (fifty micro-flags nobody remembers). Constraint: entitlements are defined in one reviewed config file, grouped in bundles (base / member / tier-N), max ~15 keys until a real commercial need forces more.

Sequencing conflict with the comms project: both designs need the same backend foundation. Build it once, portal-first or comms-first — but not as two teams in parallel inventing two account tables. My recommendation: foundation → portal MVP (identity + entitlements prove out) → comms layer plugs into it.

Awaiting approval. Natural first decisions: (a) Phase-1 scope — the trimmed five-feature base portal vs the full brief list; (b) portal-first vs comms-first foundation sequencing; (c) engage the privacy specialist on the §6 confirmation list.
