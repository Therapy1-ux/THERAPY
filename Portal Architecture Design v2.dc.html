Platform architecture v2 — THERAPY is the client system of record.

Revision of v1 per the architecture correction: Momence is no longer treated as CRM or master identity. It becomes an external transactional service — bookings, availability, scheduling, payments, membership billing — behind a replaceable integration. Nothing implemented; this supersedes v1's source-of-truth map, data boundaries, Momence integration and roadmap.

1 · Core principle (the correction)

THERAPY owns the platform and the client relationship. External vendors provide replaceable services. The THERAPY platform holds the permanent person_id and everything governed under it: identity & authentication, profile & contact details, consents & comms preferences, intake, questionnaires, assessments, goals, progress, clinician-approved health information, longitudinal Health Memory, wearable data, biomarkers/pathology, program participation, recommendations, communications history, staff follow-ups, clinical information under permission — and the future Seasons relationship.

Momence's role shrinks to transactions: appointment availability, scheduling/bookings, payments, membership billing. It exchanges only what those functions need. It is never the master identity and never a health/client database.

Replaceability test: if Momence is swapped out, THERAPY replaces one integration adapter — no data migration, no core-platform rebuild. Every design decision below must pass this test. (What changed from v1: v1 made Momence the identity and contact master with the portal "keyed to Momence customer ID". That inverted the dependency — reversed here.)

2 · Identity — THERAPY person_id ↔ Momence customer_id

The person_id is minted and owned by THERAPY at first consented interaction and never changes. External systems attach to it through an external-references domain: person_id → (system, external_id, linked_at, status). Momence's customer_id is the first such reference; Outlook contacts, wearable accounts, a future Seasons profile and any Momence replacement are all just more rows.

THERAPY person_id  ↔  external Momence customer_id
THERAPY Platform → read availability from Momence
THERAPY Platform → create / read / update bookings (where supported)
THERAPY Platform ← appointment status events
THERAPY Platform ← payment / membership transaction status

Authentication is ours (email magic-link at MVP, passkeys later) against the THERAPY identity domain — clients never log into Momence. Linking: on account creation we match/create the Momence customer via API and store the reference; unmatched or ambiguous cases queue for staff resolution rather than auto-merging.

3 · Data architecture — ten governed domains, not one database

"THERAPY owns the client data" ≠ one unrestricted store. Ten bounded domains, each with its own access rules; role-based access and least privilege apply across all of them, and every sensitive read is logged.

1 · Identity & authentication
person_id, credentials, sessions, MFA. Readable by almost nothing; auth service only.
2 · Client profile
Name, contact, preferences, demographics. Client-editable; broad staff read, audited write.
3 · Clinical / Health Memory
Notes, observations, longitudinal record spine. Clinical roles scoped to assigned clients; client sees only clinician-published records.
4 · Communications
Conversation store (per comms design), staff follow-ups. Reception + assigned staff; never joined to domain 3 without permission.
5 · Consent & permissions
Versioned consent ledger, comms preferences, sharing grants. Append-only; gates reads across all other domains.
6 · Wearables & integrations
Device links, OAuth tokens, normalised metrics feeding domain 3. Entitlement-gated (insights.wearables).
7 · Assessments & biomarkers
Questionnaires, pathology, scans, scores. Clinical governance rules of domain 3; publication is an explicit act.
8 · Audit logs
Immutable access + change log across every domain. Write-only for services; compliance-role read.
9 · External system references
person_id ↔ external ids (Momence, wearables, Seasons…). The only place vendor ids exist.
10 · Booking / payment references
Read-model mirror of Momence bookings & transaction status, provenance-tagged, rebuildable from the vendor at any time. Never the ledger of record for money.

Rule unchanged: each fact lives in exactly one system; everything else holds references. What changed: identity and contact moved from the Momence column to ours. New obligation this creates: a contact-sync policy — THERAPY is authoritative, changes write through to Momence via the adapter; Momence-side edits (front desk) surface as reconciliation events, never silently overwrite.

5 · Momence integration adapter

All Momence traffic goes through a single booking/payments adapter exposing a vendor-neutral interface to the rest of the platform: getAvailability, createBooking, updateBooking, cancelBooking, onAppointmentStatus, onTransactionStatus. No other service imports the Momence API. Webhooks land in the adapter, are translated to platform events keyed by person_id (via domain 9), then fan out to entitlements, comms and the read-model.

Scope of exchange (least data): outbound — the minimum client fields Momence needs to hold a booking/payment (name, email/phone as required); inbound — availability, booking/appointment status, membership/payment transaction status. No health data, consents, notes, or preferences ever cross this boundary.

Known constraint to verify in the sandbox: how far Momence's API supports create/update of bookings and payment initiation headlessly. Where it doesn't, the portal deep-links into a Momence-hosted step and returns the client to THERAPY on completion — an accepted Phase-1 seam, invisible-ish by styling and redirect, eliminated as API coverage allows.

6 · One experience — client and staff

Client: logs into THERAPY → dashboard → progress → membership → books an appointment. The booking is processed through the Momence adapter and the client returns to (or never leaves) the THERAPY experience. Which vendor performed which function is never the client's problem.

Staff: the THERAPY Staff Portal is the primary surface for all client workflows — profile, history, follow-ups, comms, clinical view (permissioned). Momence, Outlook and other systems operate as integrations behind it; staff drop into a vendor UI only where the adapter can't yet perform the action, and each such seam is tracked as debt to close. Long-term objective: external systems disappear behind the THERAPY interface.

7 · Entitlements, Seasons, governance — carried forward

Entitlements (v1 §2) survive intact, re-keyed to person_id: one entitlements table, bundles (portal.base / member.core / tier keys / seasons.*), Momence transaction events grant/revoke automatically, staff grants audited, ~15 keys max in one reviewed config.

Seasons (v1 §5) unchanged and strengthened by this correction: Seasons is another controlled interface on the same THERAPY identity — shared login and wearable connections, separated clinical data, nothing shared cross-entity without its own consent event.

Governance (v1 §6) unchanged: RBAC, least privilege, encryption, AU residency, immutable audit, consent ledger, retention controls; the specialist-confirmation list (Victorian Health Records Act, APP 11, retention minimums, AI-derived insights, Seasons entity structure) still gates the build. Raised stakes: owning identity/auth and contact data ourselves increases our security surface vs v1 — auth hardening and the audit domain move from "important" to "non-negotiable before any client login ships".

9 · Honest costs of this correction

Phase 0 gets heavier. v1 borrowed Momence's identity to ship faster; v2 builds identity, auth, references, consent and audit before the first login. Roughly +2–4 weeks up front — bought back permanently in vendor independence.

Two-way contact sync is real work. The moment we own contact data, front-desk edits inside Momence become a reconciliation problem. The write-through + reconciliation-event policy in §4 must exist from day one or the two systems drift.

The comms design needs a matching correction. Its "Momence is the CRM spine" framing is now wrong: Momence remains its transaction spine, but conversations key to person_id and client lookups hit our profile domain. One targeted revision, not a rewrite — flagged for the next pass on that document.
