3 · Proposed architecture

Channels
Phone/SMS (Twilio) · Outlook (Graph) · Portal chat · Web forms
Core: Comms service
One conversation store — every thread (SMS, email, call log) normalised to conversation + messages + client match
AI gateway
Model-agnostic · per-function permissions · every call logged
Systems of record
Momence (identity, bookings, payments) · Health Memory DB (clinical — separate store, separate keys)
Interfaces on top: Staff hub (unified inbox — missed calls, SMS threads, email queue, AI drafts, escalations, follow-ups; one-click human takeover freezes the AI on that thread) and Member portal. Both read the same conversation store. The Reception AI and the future Clinical Copilot are two different gateway clients with disjoint permissions — Reception AI can see contact details, membership tier, appointment history and availability; it structurally cannot query the Health Memory store (separate service, no credentials), not merely "isn't allowed to".

4 · Workflows

Missed call → SMS → AI conversation
Call unanswered (Twilio webhook)→ Number matched to Momence client?→ Auto-SMS (identifies as THERAPY's AI assistant)→ Client replies → intent classified→ Book / info / route→ Escalate to staff hub when needed
First message always: identity ("You've reached THERAPY's automated assistant"), opt-out ("reply STOP"), and a human path ("reply CALL and we'll ring you back"). Hard escalation triggers: distress or clinical language, complaints, payment disputes, anything the intent classifier scores low-confidence, and any request for clinical advice — the AI never answers those, it hands over with a summary. During business hours a missed call also pings the staff hub immediately; the SMS says when to expect a human.
Email (hello@yourtherapy.au)
Graph webhook on new mail → classify (booking / membership / clinical / supplier / other) + summarise + priority-score → match sender to Momence client only on exact verified email match (no fuzzy matching for anything sensitive) → AI writes a reply as an Outlook draft in the shared mailbox, in THERAPY's voice, with the classification and summary in an internal note → staff review, edit, send. Booking-type enquiries get availability options inserted from the Momence API. Clinical content is never drafted — flagged and routed to the right practitioner. Drafts-only is phase-1 permanent policy; auto-send for a narrow whitelist (e.g. opening-hours questions) only after months of accuracy data, and only with your sign-off.

5 · Security & governance (designed in, not bolted on)

Role-based access (member / reception staff / practitioner / admin) with least-privilege on every API scope · consent recorded at first AI contact and honoured store-wide · encryption in transit and at rest, AU-region hosting for anything health-adjacent · immutable audit log of every record view and every AI call (prompt, data accessed, output, model) · identity verification before the AI reveals anything personal over SMS/email (it can act on "book me in" without ever reading personal data back) · retention schedule per data class, deletion path when a client leaves · vendor DPAs reviewed for Twilio, Microsoft, AI provider — with AU data residency preferred · comms data and clinical data in separate stores with separate credentials. The AI never diagnoses, never gives treatment or emergency advice; emergency-language detection returns 000/Lifeline directives and escalates instantly.

7 · Where I challenge the brief

Don't build a CRM. The brief lists "CRM/client records where appropriate" — Momence already is your CRM. A second client-record store guarantees divergence. The comms layer should hold conversations only, keyed to Momence IDs.

Don't let the reception AI near identity-sensitive replies at all in phase 1. Even "least privilege" is too much until identity verification exists. Phase-1 AI should operate on intent + availability only — it can book, it cannot recite any stored personal detail.

The Clinical Copilot should not share this project's timeline. Bundling it with reception comms puts AHPRA-adjacent clinical-records risk into a receptionist tool. Design the permission boundary now (done above), build the copilot later as its own governed project.

SMS AI conversations are the highest-risk channel, not the flashiest feature. Real-time, unsupervised, with vulnerable clients possible. Recommend the AI handles exactly three intents by itself (book, hours/location, class info) and escalates everything else for the first 3 months — then widen based on transcript review.

One commercial flag: before building phone/SMS on Twilio raw, price a done-for-you AU clinic-comms platform (e.g. platforms with missed-call-text-back built in) as a stopgap. Custom build is the right end state for the one-account vision, but a $50/mo tool could capture missed calls this month while the real system is built.
